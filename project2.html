Introduction

This project analyzes sales data from a coffee shop chain to uncover trends across time, locations, and product categories. Using Excel as the primary tool, the dataset sourced from Maven Analytics was cleaned, transformed, and structured using Power Query Editor. Key performance indicators such as total sales, footfall, average bill per person, and order frequency were calculated. Interactive dashboards with slicers were developed to explore patterns and support data-driven decision-making.

Objective

  • Utilize sales data provided by Maven Analytics to analyze performance across months, hours, product categories, and store locations.
  • Perform data cleaning and transformation in Power Query, including removing inconsistencies, replacing null values, trimming text, and extracting date parts (Month, Hour).
  • Develop pivot tables and calculated fields to measure metrics such as total sales, average bill, and customer count..
  • Create an interactive Excel dashboard with dynamic filters and charts to visualize sales distribution and identify key business insights..
  • Support business strategy with actionable insights such as best-selling items, peak operating hours, and high-performing store locations.

Methodology

  1. Data Collection

    The coffee shop sales dataset was sourced from Maven Analytics. It includes transaction-level data such as date, time, store location, product details, quantity sold, price, and footfall. The data was provided in .csv format and imported into Excel for analysis.

  2. Data Loading and Transformation

    The collected .csv file is loaded into excel and then transformed into Power Query Editor from excel where data cleaning is done there.

  3. Data Cleaning

    Data set:

    • Connected to the original data file which was taken from maven analytics.
    • Accessed the relevant sheet or table.
    • Used the first row as column headers.
    • Added conditional column from product details.
    • Added custom column by calculating total sales(product of quantity and price).
    • Ensured the new columns had the correct data types.
    • Extracted the month name from the transaction dates.
    • Extracted the hour of transaction to analyze daily patterns.
    • Extracted the month number to support sorting/filtering.

  4. Data Export

    These transformations ensured clean, structured, and analysis-ready data for further calculations and examination.

  5. Measure calculations

    Key metrics calculated from the transformed data included:.

    • Total Sales (sum of Total Amount).
    • Total Footfall (sum of customer visits).
    • Average Bill per Person = Total Sales / Footfall.
    • Average Orders per Person = Total Transactions / Footfall.
    • Aggregated sales by Month, Hour, Store Location, Product category and type

  6. Data Visualization

    All visualizations were built using Excel Pivot Tables, Pivot Charts, and Slicers for interactivity and relevant insights are delivered from each visual.

  7. Analysis

    1. Monthly Sales Trend

    This chart shows how total sales increased month by month. Sales grew steadily from January to June, indicating rising demand and possibly effective marketing or seasonal factors driving higher revenue each month.

    2. Hourly Sales throughout the Day

    This chart shows customer purchasing behavior by hour. Sales peak sharply between 8 AM and 10 AM, highlighting the strong morning rush when customers buy coffee and breakfast items. After 12 PM, sales gradually decline and are lowest in the evening, pointing to lower footfall later in the day.

    3. Sales Distribution by Product Type

    This chart breaks down sales by product type. Premium Beans account for the largest share (34%), showing they are the top seller. Organic and Gourmet Beans follow, while Clothing and House Blend Beans contribute smaller but still notable shares. This highlights which product lines generate the most revenue.

    4. Sales Trend by Category

    This chart compares total sales by broader product categories. Coffee is the top-selling category by far, followed by Tea and Bakery items. Drinks, Coffee Beans, and other packaged or branded items contribute less. This reveals where sales are concentrated and where there may be upselling opportunities.

    5. Sales Distribution by Store Location

    This chart shows how total sales are distributed across the three store branches. The sales are fairly balanced among Hell’s Kitchen, Astoria, and Lower Manhattan, with Hell’s Kitchen slightly leading. This indicates consistent performance across locations.

    6. Sales & Transactions by Store Location

    This combined bar chart compares total sales amount and transaction count by store. Hell’s Kitchen has the highest sales and transaction volume, followed by Astoria and Lower Manhattan. This confirms that Hell’s Kitchen has the strongest customer activity overall.

Results

    1.Sales Trends:

    • Sales consistently increased month-over-month..
    • The highest daily sales occurred between 8 AM and 10 AM, indicating a strong morning rush.
    • Sales dropped significantly after noon, suggesting a focus opportunity on afternoon/evening promotions.

    2.Category Performance:

    • Coffee and Tea were the top-selling categories.
    • Bakery and Drinks contributed moderately, while Packaged items had the least impact.

    3.Product Type Insights:

    • Premium Beans had the highest contribution (34%), followed by Organic and Gourmet Beans.
    • Clothing and House Blend Beans were niche contributors (14% each).

    4.Store-Level Analysis:

    • Hell’s Kitchen recorded the highest total revenue and transaction count.
    • Astoria and Lower Manhattan followed closely with similar performance.
    • All three locations showed balanced revenue distribution, indicating well-managed operations across branches.

    5.KPI Summary:

    • Total Sales: $698,812.33.
    • Total Footfall: 149,116.
    • Avg Bill per Person: $4.69.
    • Avg Orders per Person: 1.44.
